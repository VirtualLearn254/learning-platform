## How accounting is taught (the pedagogy)

- **Worked examples are the spine.** Every concept beat earns a companion
  example beat with REAL numbers a learner could recompute by hand. Prefer
  one running scenario per lesson (one business, one person) threading all
  examples — new numbers each beat, same world.
- **Both sides, always**: every transaction shown lands somewhere — a debit
  has its credit, an expense hits an account. Never show one leg.
- **Round-trip the intuition**: introduce the mechanical rule, then the
  "why the books stay balanced" intuition, then a case where naive intuition
  fails (the classic misconception fodder for check beats).
- Numbers discipline: realistic magnitudes, consistent currency, totals that
  actually add up — a wrong sum is a P0-equivalent correctness fail.

## Visual language

- Native forms: T-accounts, ledgers, journal-entry rows, bracket waterfalls
  (tax/tiers), pie/percent splits, compounding staircases, balance-scale
  motifs for the accounting equation.
- Show the CHANGE: highlight the delta row/cell, not the whole table.
  Tables enter row-by-row in narration sync, never all at once.
- Style: clean fintech-editorial — paper/ledger textures welcome, cartoon
  coins and money-bag clip-art never.

## Narration register

Practitioner-teacher: someone who has closed real books, talking to an adult
who will do this for real. Anchor abstractions in consequence ("get this
wrong and your balance sheet lies to you"). Define every term at first use
(dissect-and-define), and re-anchor recalled terms with their plain meaning.

## Quiz distribution (variety within the subject)

Lean on: fill_in with numericValue+tolerance (computation), sort_into
(debit/credit, asset/liability), ordering (closing process, cycle steps),
scenario (judgment calls), estimate (magnitude intuition). Every computation
quiz's wrong options are the ARITHMETIC of a specific mistake (sign flip,
wrong base, skipped step) — not random numbers.
