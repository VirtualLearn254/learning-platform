# Grade 10 Chemistry — KICD syllabus spine (course-outline reference)

Source: KICD *Senior School Curriculum Design, Grade 10, Chemistry*, June 2024,
ISBN 978-9914-52-913-5 (STEM pathway; 180 lessons/year; a lesson = 40 min).
This is the authoritative topic spine — outline lessons against THESE strands,
sub-strands, and specific learning outcomes (SLOs). Framing is
competency-based: each sub-strand also carries a Key Inquiry Question (KIQ),
core competencies, values, and Pertinent & Contemporary Issues (PCIs), and is
assessed by rubric level (Exceeds / Meets / Approaches / Below expectation).

## STRAND 1.0 — INORGANIC CHEMISTRY

### 1.1 Introduction to Chemistry (6)
Scope: meaning of chemistry; branches; careers (and how gender stereotyping
skews career choice); chemistry in daily life (agriculture, pharma, medicine,
manufacturing, food, energy, nuclear, sport); drug & substance use (dosage,
prescription); rights & responsibilities to a safe learning environment.
SLOs: explain chemistry as a field of science; explore its role in daily life;
examine effects of drug & substance use; promote a safe, healthy learning
environment. KIQ: How is the study of Chemistry important in our society?
Project: posters sensitising peers/community on drug & substance-abuse risks.

### 1.2 The Atom (24)
Scope: structure of the atom (energy levels AND orbitals); isotopes; **s and p
orbital notation for the first 20 elements**.
SLOs: describe atomic structure; determine relative atomic mass (RAM) from
isotopic abundance; write electron arrangement in **s/p notation**; develop
interest in atomic structure. KIQ: How are electrons arranged in an atom?
Watch-fors: RAM calculation weighting; orbital-fill order; s/p superscripts.
Project: model atoms from local materials. New vs 8-4-4: orbital notation
(8-4-4 used only 2.8.8 shells).

### 1.3 The Periodic Table (28)
Scope: development (groups & periods) from electron arrangement; ion formation
(cation/anion), valency vs oxidation number, radicals; deriving formulae;
balanced equations; naming the families (alkali, alkaline-earth, halogens,
noble, transition).
SLOs: relate position to electron arrangement; illustrate ion formation; derive
formulae; write balanced equations; appreciate the role of electron arrangement.
KIQ: Why is the study of the periodic table important? Watch-fors: ion charge
superscripts; valency vs oxidation number; equation balancing.

### 1.4 Chemical Bonding (24)
Scope: valence electrons, octet/duplet; bond types — **ionic, covalent, dative
(coordinate), hydrogen, Van der Waals, metallic**; Lewis dot-and-cross;
structure→property experiments (giant ionic, simple molecular, giant covalent/
atomic, giant metallic: solubility, conductivity, m.p./b.p.); NaCl, diamond,
graphite, SiO₂, Al examples.
SLOs: illustrate bond types; investigate bond-type ↔ physical-property links;
relate structure to use; appreciate uses by bond type. KIQ: How do chemical
bonds influence the properties of substances? Project: model bonding in NaCl,
SiO₂, graphite, diamond from local materials.

### 1.5 Periodicity (32 — largest sub-strand)
Scope: gradation in atomic/ionic size, appearance, ionisation energy, electron
affinity, m.p./b.p., ductility/malleability/conductivity; Groups I, II, VII,
VIII; Period 3 trends and reactions.
SLOs: describe physical-property trends; investigate chemical properties within
a group; describe trends across a period; outline applications; appreciate
them. KIQs: Why is the study of chemical families important? How does position
influence properties? Practicals: reactions of Group I & II with O₂, Cl₂, cold
water, steam, dilute acids; **prepare chlorine gas** (lab-safety PCI); Cl₂/Br₂/I₂
properties and displacement; Period-3 element reactions.

Strand 1 rubric indicators: importance of chemistry; s/p electron arrangement
of the 20 elements; balanced equations; bond-type↔property investigation;
group/period trends.

## STRAND 2.0 — PHYSICAL CHEMISTRY

### 2.1 Acids and Bases (28)
Scope: properties of acids & bases (reactions with metals, carbonates,
hydrogen carbonates, metal oxides, hydroxides); indicators; universal indicator
& pH scale; **strong vs weak** via pH and electrical conductivity (compare
equal-concentration H₂SO₄, HCl, ethanoic acid, NaOH, Na₂CO₃, ammonia);
amphoteric oxides/hydroxides; applications.
SLOs: explain characteristics in aqueous solution; describe chemical properties;
classify strong/weak by universal indicator; outline uses; appreciate uses.
KIQ: What common household substances are acidic and/or basic? PCI: lab safety.

### 2.2 Introduction to Salts (30)
Scope: types (normal, acidic, basic, double); solubility of chlorides,
carbonates, nitrates, sulphates; **preparation** — direct synthesis; acid+metal;
acid+base; acid+carbonate/hydrogen carbonate; precipitation (ionic equations
limited to precipitation); behaviour in air — **hygroscopic, deliquescent,
efflorescent**; applications (agriculture, food, medicine, paper, paints,
glass, laundry); fertiliser eutrophication & mitigation.
SLOs: classify salts; prepare salts by appropriate methods; describe behaviour
in air; outline & appreciate applications. KIQs: How do salts behave in air?
How can salts be prepared in the lab? PCI: waste management, eutrophication.

Strand 2 rubric indicators: chemical properties of acids/bases; preparing salts
by appropriate laboratory methods.

## STRAND 3.0 — ORGANIC CHEMISTRY (8) — under-specified, verify

The official summary allocates 8 lessons to Organic Chemistry, but the June
2024 design body carries **no sub-strand detail** for it. Third-party sources
describe it only as an **introduction to carbon-containing compounds**. Organic
chemistry proper develops in Grade 11. Treat this as a brief end-of-year
on-ramp (what makes carbon special; carbon compounds around us); do NOT invent
detailed alkane/alkene mechanism content here — that is Grade 11+. Re-verify if
an updated KICD edition surfaces.

## Appendix (official)

Resources: conventional lab + improvised local materials + digital/online.
Assessment methods: Q&A, written exercises, individual performance assessment,
observation, project work, practicals. Non-formal: excursions, field work,
science clubs, symposia, games.

## Not in Grade 10 (deferred to Grade 11–12) — do not pull these in

The mole & quantitative chemistry, gas laws, water & hydrogen, air &
combustion, energy changes/thermochemistry, reaction rates, metals & extraction,
full organic chemistry, electrochemistry/electrolysis. (Several are 8-4-4 Form
1–3 staples — the KLB Form 2 book includes electrolysis and carbon, which are
NOT in CBC Grade 10. Ignore the book; follow this spine.)
