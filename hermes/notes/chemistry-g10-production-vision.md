# Grade 10 Chemistry — production vision (draft for notes exchange)

Status: Claude's draft, 2026-08-08. To be reconciled with the producer's own
notes before it hardens into the profile's production instructions.

The one-sentence thesis: **teach the invisible through the visible** — every
lesson builds a particle-level mental model the learner can SEE move, anchored
to observables they could meet at a real bench, narrated like a master teacher
who has done the experiment, at a pace that lets understanding settle.

---

## 1. What "pedagogically superior" means here (the standard)

The bar is not "better than other videos." It is **better than a very good
human teacher with a whiteboard**, which means the video must do what that
teacher cannot:

1. **Show the invisible in motion.** A teacher draws a static shell diagram;
   we animate the electron leaving sodium and arriving at chlorine, watch the
   charge imbalance appear, and see the lattice assemble. Every abstraction
   in chemistry is a moving picture badly served by chalk — that is our edge.
2. **Perfect recall of the thread.** Every recalled term is re-anchored
   ("remember, an ion is just an atom that's lost or gained electrons") and
   every new idea is placed on the scaffold of the previous ones. No human
   holds this discipline for 180 lessons; the author skill enforces it.
3. **Exhaustive first-principles depth, zero fluff.** Narration teaches WHY
   at every step (why does sodium lose rather than gain seven? energy), never
   defines-and-moves-on, never pads. 60–100+ words of digesting narration per
   beat; depth supersedes the cap when the concept demands it.
4. **Patience engineered in.** ≤0.90 narration speed, settle moments after
   each reveal, 3–4 sub-scenes per beat each ≥8s, visual complete ≥1s before
   audio ends. Teacher pacing, not reel pacing.

## 2. The learner (assumptions)

- **Kenyan Grade 10 STEM-pathway learner, ~15–16**, first CBC senior cohort
  (2026). They CHOSE chemistry — treat them as motivated adults-in-training:
  adult editorial register, zero cartoon energy, but vocabulary introduced
  with dissect-and-define care (English is often their 2nd/3rd language).
- Their assessment world is **rubrics + school-based assessment + inquiry**,
  not a single terminal exam. So videos teach toward *"can investigate,
  illustrate, explain"* — the KICD verbs — not toward recall tricks.
- Many are in schools with **thin lab access**. Videos must make experiments
  vivid and honest (real apparatus, real colours, real hazards) while being
  clear they complement, never replace, touching the real thing.
- Kenyan anchors are identity, not decoration: Magadi soda ash, fertiliser
  and eutrophication, jua-kali metalwork for malleability, the lilac of
  potassium in a school demo.

## 3. Source-of-truth hierarchy (what drives what)

1. **KICD Grade 10 design (June 2024)** — decides WHAT to teach, in what
   framing (SLOs, KIQs, competencies, projects). The syllabus spine file in
   pedagogy-chemistry is the operational copy.
2. **First-principles chemistry** — decides HOW it is explained. Narration is
   authored from understanding, never adapted from any textbook's prose.
3. **KLB Form 2 chapters (private reference/)** — fact-checking only:
   values, conventions, standard examples' correctness. Known to carry small
   errors; trust but verify. Never a source of phrasing or structure.
4. **Profile memory** — what landed with learners accumulates and outranks
   habit.

## 4. Course architecture

- **Unit = KICD sub-strand** (8 units). **Lesson = one coherent conceptual
  arc** within it, sized to one SLO-cluster — typically 5–8 minutes of video,
  6–10 beats. A 24-lesson sub-strand like The Atom becomes ~5–6 videos, not 24.
- **Beat grammar per lesson** (flexible, not a template):
  1. *Hook/KIQ beat* — open from the sub-strand's Key Inquiry Question or a
     concrete puzzle ("chlorine's RAM is 35.5 — how can half a neutron
     exist?").
  2. *Build beats* — one idea each, particles-first then symbols, claim →
     unpack → example → land.
  3. *Worked-example beat* — real numbers on screen, voice walks the eye.
  4. *Check beat* — quiz whose wrong options are the chemistry of a specific
     misconception (see §6), with LP-18 remediation branch for the classic one.
  5. *Settle beat* — place the idea on the growing map; set up the next
     lesson's question.
- **Continuity spine**: one growing visual world per unit — e.g. the periodic
  table as a persistent "map" that fills in as the course progresses, the
  same atom-model style throughout. Recall = revisit the same visual, not a
  new one.
- **Projects and practicals** get their own beat treatment: animate the KICD
  project brief (bonding models from local materials, drug-abuse posters) as
  a call-to-do, not a lecture.
- **Per lesson package**: MP4 beats + quizzes + branches + LP-19 notes PDF
  (the notes double as the revision text the learner keeps) + SCORM zip.

## 5. Visual approach and style

- **Style family: clean lab-editorial.** Paper-white or deep-slate scenes,
  one accent for "the thing that matters now," real molecular geometry, real
  apparatus silhouettes. Never googly-eyed atoms, bubbling-green-flask
  clip-art, or comic fonts. It should feel like a beautifully produced
  Nature-education explainer, tuned for a 15-year-old's patience.
- **One visual idea per sub-scene**, entrance → hero → settle; motion is
  meaning (an electron MOVES from Na to Cl; a trend GROWS down the group) —
  never decorative drift.
- **Chemistry-native device set** (to be built/distilled into LP-20 palette as
  they prove out):
  - *periodic-strip* — group/period slice that highlights members one by one
    as a trend builds; the full-table "map" variant for course continuity.
  - *shell-orbital-atom* — Bohr shells with s/p orbital-fill overlay, the
    2.8.1 ↔ 1s²2s²2p⁶3s¹ dual notation shown as two views of one truth.
  - *dot-cross-bond* — ionic transfer / covalent share animation.
  - *electrolysis-cell / gas-prep rig* — labelled apparatus with ion-migration
    or gas-path arrows.
  - *ph-scale* — universal-indicator gradient with markers.
  - *reaction-panel* — before/after observation card (colour change, gas
    test) with the balanced equation assembling beneath.
  - Plus the existing generic devices where they map (flow-from-sources,
    split-mirror, delta-chip, myth-truth-panels, settle-check).
- **Formula fidelity is a P0 visual gate**: every subscript, superscript,
  charge sign, state symbol, and coefficient read character-by-character at
  frame QA. H₂SO₄ rendered flat is a failed beat, same severity as overlap.
- **On-screen text supports, never transcribes** — key nouns, numbers,
  formulae; the narration carries the teaching.

## 6. Misconception engineering (the quiz doctrine)

Chemistry has a well-mapped misconception landscape; quizzes target it
deliberately, and remediation branches (LP-18) are written for the classic
ones. Grade-10 examples the check beats aim at:

- Mass number vs atomic number vs RAM confusion; "RAM 35.5 means a chlorine
  atom weighs 35.5."
- Subscript vs coefficient (2H₂O has 4 H, not 2); balancing by changing a
  formula instead of a coefficient.
- Charge sign/magnitude errors (Na⁺ vs Na⁻; Mg²⁺ vs Mg⁺²-as-notation).
- "Atoms want full shells" as agency vs energy reasoning (why Na loses 1
  rather than gains 7).
- Ionic bonding as "sharing" / covalent as "transfer" swap; molecules of NaCl.
- Strong acid = concentrated acid conflation; pH as "amount" of acid.
- Solubility overgeneralisation ("all salts dissolve"); hygroscopic vs
  deliquescent vs efflorescent mix-ups.

Wrong options are always the *result of the specific mistake*, never random.

## 7. Production mechanics (who does what)

- **Hermes chemistry profile** (brain TBD at production time) conducts:
  outlines lessons against the KICD spine, authors narration + visual specs
  (naming LP-20 devices), writes quizzes and branch beats, judges rendered
  frames (formula fidelity added to the standard QA), files correction notes,
  escalates to the human gate. KLB reference consulted only at fact-check.
- **The app substrate** executes deterministically: TTS with Whisper word
  timestamps driving sub-scene sync, designer AI (GLM 5.2 today; K3 slot
  ready) composing HTML/GSAP from the device palette, HyperFrames rendering,
  stitching, notes PDF, SCORM packaging.
- **Human gate (you)** approves each beat before publish; corrections flow
  back as memory + skill amendments (two-layer learning: subject lessons stay
  in profile memory, craft lessons become repo skill PRs).
- **Smoke-test choice**: Sub-strand 1.2 (The Atom) first — RAM calculations
  and dual notation stress formula fidelity and the worked-example machinery
  hardest; if it holds there, the rest of the course is downhill.
